## Important Distinction

The article should not treat every use of "prediction error" as
referring to the same biological phenomenon.

Distinguish:

- perceptual prediction error;
- reward prediction error;
- computational prediction error;
- particular neural signals associated with prediction-error-like
  computations.

Do not claim:

"Dopamine is the brain's prediction-error signal."

The biological relationship is more complicated.

---

## Psychosis Connection

The article should ask whether altered prediction-error processing could
contribute to psychosis without assuming that it does.

Questions include:

- Could abnormal weighting of prediction errors alter perception?
- Could unusual prediction errors contribute to aberrant salience?
- Could persistent unexplained prediction errors influence belief
  formation?
- Could altered precision change which prediction errors are treated
  as important?

Avoid:

"Schizophrenia is caused by abnormal prediction error."

Prefer:

"Prediction-error models provide one computational framework for
investigating some phenomena associated with psychosis."

## PIF Connection

Potential conceptual chain:

Prediction
↓
Prediction error
↓
Precision weighting
↓
Learning / updating
↓
Perception and belief
↓
Experience

The Precision Instability Framework asks whether instability in
precision maintenance or allocation could produce unstable treatment of
prediction errors across different domains.

PIF remains a proposed research hypothesis.

The article should establish the conceptual foundation rather than
presenting PIF as validated.

## Evidence Hierarchy

### Relatively Established

- Organisms learn from discrepancies between expected and observed
  outcomes.
- Prediction errors can drive updating.
- Context and uncertainty influence learning.
- Prediction-error-like computations appear in multiple domains.

### Well-Developed Computational Frameworks

- Predictive coding.
- Reinforcement-learning prediction errors.
- Bayesian belief updating.

### Active Research Questions

- How prediction errors are represented neurally.
- How precision controls their influence.
- Whether apparently similar neural signals represent the same
  computation.
- How prediction-error mechanisms relate to psychosis.

### Not Established

- One universal prediction-error mechanism.
- Prediction error as the sole mechanism of psychosis.
- A simple equation between dopamine and prediction error.
- A single abnormality explaining schizophrenia.

## Closing Transition

Article 4 asked:

"What happens when the prediction is wrong?"

Article 5 should establish that the mismatch itself is not enough.

The brain also has to determine:

"How much should this mismatch matter?"

This leads naturally to the concept of precision.
