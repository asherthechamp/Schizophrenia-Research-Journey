# Article 07 — Curriculum Plan

## Title

How Does the Brain Learn When the World Is Changing?

## Subtitle

Uncertainty, volatility, and the computational problem of belief updating

## Core question

How does the brain distinguish temporary uncertainty from a genuinely changing environment?

## Schizophrenia connection

Investigate how uncertainty, inferred volatility, prediction-error weighting,
and learning rates may contribute to differences in belief updating and
psychotic experiences.

Do not reduce schizophrenia or psychosis to a single computational parameter.

## PIF connection

PIF should remain a proposed hypothesis.

Article 7 should establish uncertainty and volatility before connecting them
to possible precision dynamics.

Future questions include whether precision is appropriately coupled to
uncertainty and whether precision/uncertainty dynamics become unstable over
time.

## Empirical direction

Potential approaches:

- probabilistic learning tasks;
- computational modeling;
- behavioral measures of belief updating;
- fMRI;
- EEG/MEG;
- longitudinal or trial-by-trial analysis.

## Scientific constraints

Do not claim:

- volatility equals psychosis;
- high learning rate equals schizophrenia;
- low learning rate equals delusion;
- uncertainty causes psychosis;
- PIF is established.
