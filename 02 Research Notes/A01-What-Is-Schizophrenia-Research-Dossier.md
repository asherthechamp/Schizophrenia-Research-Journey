# Article 01 Research Dossier

## Title

What Is Schizophrenia? Why One of Psychiatry's Most Important Diagnoses Is So Difficult to Define

## Research Question

What exactly is schizophrenia, and why has psychiatry and neuroscience not identified a single biological mechanism that defines the disorder?

## Core Findings

### 1. Clinical diagnosis

Schizophrenia remains primarily a clinical diagnosis.

There is currently no single validated biological test that independently defines schizophrenia in routine clinical practice.

### 2. Symptom dimensions

Schizophrenia can involve:

- Hallucinations
- Delusions
- Disorganized thinking
- Disorganized behavior
- Negative symptoms
- Cognitive difficulties
- Disturbances of agency and self-experience

### 3. Heterogeneity

People diagnosed with schizophrenia can differ substantially in symptom profile, illness course, cognition, functioning, genetic risk, environmental exposure, and treatment response.

### 4. Genetics

Large genomic studies demonstrate complex genetic architecture involving many common variants and rarer higher-impact variants.

### 5. Biomarkers

Many candidate biomarkers have been investigated, but no single biomarker currently provides a definitive clinical diagnosis.

### 6. Multiple explanatory levels

Schizophrenia research can be organized across:

Genes → Development → Cells/Synapses → Circuits → Computation → Psychology → Experience → Behavior

This is a conceptual research map, not a proven single causal chain.

## Important Distinction

Diagnosis ≠ mechanism.

A clinically useful diagnostic category does not necessarily correspond to one biological disease entity.

## PIF Status

The Precision Instability Framework is a proposed hypothesis developed within this project.

It is NOT an established theory of schizophrenia.

Any discussion of PIF must explicitly preserve this distinction.
