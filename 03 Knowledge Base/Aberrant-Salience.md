# Aberrant Salience

## Definition

Aberrant salience refers to the hypothesis that otherwise neutral
stimuli or events may acquire inappropriate or excessive significance.

The concept has been influential in computational and neurobiological
accounts of psychosis.

---

## Dopamine

The aberrant-salience hypothesis has historically emphasized abnormal
dopaminergic signaling.

Dopamine is thought to contribute to the assignment of motivational or
behavioral significance to events.

However, dopamine has multiple functions and should not be reduced to a
single "salience signal."

---

## Prediction Error Connection

Computational accounts have connected aberrant salience with
prediction-error and learning mechanisms.

A simplified hypothesis is:

Unexpected event
↓
Prediction-error-related processing
↓
Dopaminergic signaling
↓
Abnormally increased significance
↓
Aberrant salience

This is a conceptual model, not an established complete mechanism.

---

## Psychosis

Aberrant salience has been proposed as a mechanism contributing to
psychotic experiences.

Potential phenomena include:

- unusual significance assigned to neutral events;
- altered interpretation of environmental cues;
- unusual motivational significance;
- possible contributions to delusional meaning-making.

---

## Important Limitation

The simple equation:

Psychosis = excessive dopamine = prediction error

is scientifically inadequate.

Modern research considers interactions among:

- dopamine;
- prediction;
- uncertainty;
- precision;
- learning;
- context;
- cognition;
- individual differences.

---

## Relationship to PIF

PIF may eventually examine whether instability in precision allocation
could contribute to unstable salience assignment.

This is a proposed research direction.

It should not be treated as evidence that PIF is already established.

---

## Scientific Status

Aberrant salience is an influential hypothesis in psychosis research.

Its precise computational and neural mechanisms remain under
investigation.
