# Precision

## Definition

In probabilistic and predictive-processing frameworks, precision is
commonly associated with the inverse of uncertainty or variance.

A simplified relationship is:

Precision ≈ 1 / variance

Higher precision implies greater estimated reliability.

Lower precision implies greater estimated uncertainty.

---

## Why Precision Matters

A prediction error is not necessarily treated as equally important in
every situation.

The system may estimate how reliable the information is.

Conceptually:

Prediction error
×
Precision
=
Precision-weighted prediction error

This provides a mechanism for determining how strongly discrepancies
should influence inference or updating.

---

## Example

Suppose two sensory signals conflict with an existing prediction.

Signal A:

Large discrepancy + low reliability

Signal B:

Smaller discrepancy + high reliability

A computational system may give Signal B greater influence despite its
smaller raw discrepancy.

Therefore:

Magnitude of error ≠ computational influence

---

## Predictive Processing

Precision is important in predictive-processing theories because it can
determine the relative influence of:

- sensory evidence;
- prior predictions;
- prediction errors;
- contextual information.

This provides a computational connection between uncertainty,
attention, inference, and learning.

---

## Precision and Attention

Precision and attention are related but should not be treated as
identical.

Some predictive-processing accounts propose that attention can be
understood partly in terms of changing the precision assigned to
information.

However:

Precision ≠ attention

Attention is a broader psychological and neural phenomenon.

---

## Precision and Psychosis

Predictive-processing accounts of psychosis have proposed that altered
precision weighting could contribute to abnormal inference.

Potential possibilities include:

- excessive precision assigned to priors;
- insufficient precision assigned to sensory evidence;
- excessive precision assigned to sensory prediction errors;
- unstable precision across contexts or time.

These are hypotheses, not established explanations of schizophrenia.

---

## Precision Instability Framework

The Precision Instability Framework (PIF) proposes that instability in
precision maintenance or allocation could contribute to disturbances
across multiple psychological domains.

Potential domains include:

- perception;
- attention;
- salience;
- belief updating;
- motivation;
- agency.

PIF is a proposed research hypothesis.

It requires operational definitions, measurable parameters, empirical
testing, comparison with alternative models, and falsifiable
predictions.

---

## Important Questions

Can precision instability be distinguished from:

- consistently low precision?
- consistently high precision?
- incorrect precision calibration?
- noisy inference?
- unstable attention?

These questions are central to developing PIF into a testable model.

---

## Scientific Status

Precision is a well-established mathematical concept in probability
and statistics.

Its specific interpretation as a neural computational quantity is an
active area of research.

The exact biological implementation of precision weighting remains
under investigation.
