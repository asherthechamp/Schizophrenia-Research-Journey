# Reward Prediction Error

## Definition

Reward prediction error is the discrepancy between an expected reward
and the reward actually received.

A simplified formulation is:

Reward prediction error
=
received reward − expected reward

---

## Positive Prediction Error

When:

Received reward > expected reward

the reward prediction error is positive.

This can signal that an outcome was better than expected.

---

## Negative Prediction Error

When:

Received reward < expected reward

the reward prediction error is negative.

This can signal that an outcome was worse than expected.

---

## Zero Prediction Error

When:

Received reward ≈ expected reward

there is little new reward information for updating the relevant value
estimate.

---

## Reinforcement Learning

Reward prediction error is central to temporal-difference learning and
other reinforcement-learning models.

A prediction error can function as a teaching signal for updating
estimates of value.

---

## Dopamine

Phasic responses of some dopamine neurons are consistent with
reward-prediction-error-like computations.

Classic findings include:

- responses to unexpected rewards;
- transfer of responses toward predictive cues after learning;
- reduced responses when an expected reward is omitted.

However:

Dopamine ≠ reward prediction error

Dopamine systems are heterogeneous and participate in multiple
functions.

---

## Difference From Sensory Prediction Error

Reward prediction error concerns expected versus received reward or
value.

Sensory prediction error concerns expected versus observed sensory
information.

They share a computational motif but should not be assumed to represent
the same neural mechanism.

---

## Psychosis

Reward prediction error and dopamine signaling are relevant to
computational theories of psychosis and aberrant salience.

However, the relationship is complex.

Psychosis should not be reduced to:

dopamine → prediction error → schizophrenia
