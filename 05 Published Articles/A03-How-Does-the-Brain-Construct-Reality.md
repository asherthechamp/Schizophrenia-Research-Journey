## Core Question

How does the brain construct perceptual experience from incomplete,
uncertain sensory evidence?

## Conceptual Progression

Diagnosis → Experience → Perception → Computation

Article 3 moves the research journey from phenomenology toward
computational neuroscience.

It introduces:

- sensory evidence;
- Bayesian inference;
- prediction;
- prediction error;
- uncertainty;
- precision;
- predictive processing;
- hallucinations;
- delusions;
- aberrant salience;
- computational psychiatry.

## Relationship to PIF

The article introduces the Precision Instability Framework (PIF) as
a proposed research hypothesis.

PIF is explicitly NOT presented as an established theory of
schizophrenia.

The central research question is whether instability in precision
maintenance or allocation could contribute to disturbances across
multiple domains.

## Scientific Position

The article distinguishes established scientific findings from
theoretical frameworks and original hypotheses.

Predictive processing, Bayesian inference, and aberrant-salience
approaches are treated as active areas of research rather than
complete explanations of schizophrenia.

PIF remains an original hypothesis requiring empirical testing and
comparison with competing explanations.

## Research Principle

The purpose of the research journey is not to force schizophrenia
into one theory.

It is to distinguish:

**What we know**

from

**What we hypothesize**

and then ask which hypotheses survive empirical evidence.
