## Article Summary

Article 04 introduces predictive processing as a computational
framework for understanding how the brain may combine sensory evidence,
prior information, predictions, context, and uncertainty to construct
perceptual interpretations.

The article emphasizes that predictive processing should not be
interpreted as the brain simply inventing reality.

Instead, perception is presented as a process of constrained inference
in which sensory evidence and internally generated predictions interact.

The article introduces:

- sensory evidence;
- predictions;
- generative models;
- hierarchical processing;
- top-down and bottom-up processing;
- prediction error;
- Bayesian inference;
- precision;
- predictive processing and psychosis;
- hallucinations and delusions;
- the limits of current evidence.

The article also introduces the relationship between predictive
processing and the Precision Instability Framework (PIF), while
explicitly maintaining that PIF is a proposed research hypothesis and
not an established scientific theory.

## Scientific Position

The article distinguishes between:

### Established or relatively well-supported observations

- Sensory information is incomplete and uncertain.
- Context and prior knowledge can influence perception.
- Prediction-related processes occur in the nervous system.
- Unexpected information can influence learning and behavior.

### Influential theoretical frameworks

- Predictive coding.
- Predictive processing.
- Hierarchical generative models.
- Bayesian approaches to perception.

### Active research questions

- How predictive-processing computations are implemented in neural
  circuits.
- What particular neural signals represent.
- How precision is biologically implemented.
- How predictive mechanisms contribute to psychiatric symptoms.

### Not established

- Predictive processing as a complete theory of brain function.
- Predictive processing as a complete explanation of schizophrenia.
- Hallucinations as simply the result of overly strong priors.
- One predictive-processing abnormality explaining all psychotic
  symptoms.

---

## PIF Connection

Article 04 introduces the conceptual relationship between predictive
processing and the Precision Instability Framework.

Predictive processing asks how a nervous system can infer the causes of
sensory input using predictions, evidence, and uncertainty.

PIF asks whether instability in the estimation, maintenance, or
allocation of precision could contribute to disturbances across
multiple psychological domains.

PIF remains a proposed research hypothesis requiring empirical testing.
