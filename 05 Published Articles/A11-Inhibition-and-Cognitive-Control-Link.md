## Summary

Article 11 examines inhibition as a component of cognitive control. It distinguishes action restraint from action cancellation, explores Go/No-Go and Stop-Signal paradigms, reactive and proactive control, selective stopping, interference control, working memory, and the distributed neural systems involved in regulating behavior.

The article connects inhibition to the broader progression:

**Attention → Filtering → Inhibition → Cognitive Control**

It also examines inhibitory-control findings in schizophrenia while avoiding the oversimplification that schizophrenia is simply an "inhibition deficit."

## Key Distinctions

- Attention → What deserves priority?
- Filtering → What should receive less priority?
- Inhibition → What should be constrained or stopped?
- Cognitive Control → How are priorities maintained in pursuit of a goal?

## Central Conceptual Statement

> The brain does not only need to decide what to do. It also needs to decide what not to do, when to stop, what to suppress, what to maintain, and when to change course.
