## Central Scientific Distinctions

**Consciousness**

> There is subjective experience.

**Self**

> Experience is organized from a first-person perspective and can carry a sense of mineness.

**Agency**

> An action or event is experienced as generated or controlled by oneself.

The article emphasizes:

**Consciousness ≠ Selfhood ≠ Agency**

## Schizophrenia Connection

The article does not describe schizophrenia simply as a disorder of consciousness or a disorder of the self.

Instead, it examines evidence that some aspects of basic self-experience—including agency, body ownership, and subjective self-experience—can differ in schizophrenia.

It also introduces phenomenological concepts such as hyperreflexivity and diminished self-affection while treating them as theoretical frameworks for describing particular experiences rather than complete causal explanations of schizophrenia.

## Neurophenomenological Bridge

A central idea is:

**First-person description ↔ Computational characterization ↔ Neural measurement**

The article argues that understanding psychiatric experience requires connecting phenomenology, cognitive science, computational models, and neuroscience without collapsing one explanatory level into another.

## Central Message

Consciousness concerns subjective experience.

Ordinary conscious experience is normally organized from a particular point of view.

That experience can carry a sense of mineness, embodiment, and agency.

The self may therefore be understood as a layered organization rather than a single entity or brain region.

Understanding disturbances of self-experience requires studying these layers separately and examining how they interact.
