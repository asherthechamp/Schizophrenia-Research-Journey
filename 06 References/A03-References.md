# I. Foundational Predictive Coding and Perception

## 1. Rao & Ballard (1999)

Rao, R. P. N., & Ballard, D. H. (1999).

**Predictive coding in the visual cortex: a functional interpretation
of some extra-classical receptive-field effects.**

Nature Neuroscience, 2, 79–87.

DOI: 10.1038/4580

https://doi.org/10.1038/4580

PMID: N/A

### Role in Article

Foundational computational model of predictive coding in visual
processing.

The paper proposes a hierarchical model in which feedback carries
predictions and feedforward signals carry residual errors.

### Evidence Status

**Foundational computational model**

Not evidence that schizophrenia is caused by predictive coding.

# VIII. Important Scientific Distinctions

## Predictive Processing

A family of theoretical and computational approaches concerning
prediction, inference, and hierarchical processing.

## Bayesian Inference

A mathematical framework for updating beliefs using evidence and
prior information.

## Prediction Error

A representation of discrepancy between predicted and incoming
information within particular computational models.

## Precision

A computational quantity related to the reliability or inverse
uncertainty of information.

## Aberrant Salience

A framework proposing that inappropriate stimuli can acquire
excessive significance, with dopamine playing an important role
in the theory.

## Psychosis

A clinical syndrome involving phenomena including hallucinations,
delusions, and disorganization.

## PIF

The **Precision Instability Framework** is an original proposed
research hypothesis of the Schizophrenia Research Journey.

It is NOT an established theory of schizophrenia.

# IX. Evidence Governance for Article 03

Article 03 must not claim:

- that predictive processing explains all perception;
- that predictive processing explains schizophrenia;
- that hallucinations are simply excessive priors;
- that delusions are simply excessive priors;
- that dopamine is identical to precision;
- that prediction error has one universal neural implementation;
- that aberrant salience explains all psychosis;
- that PIF has been experimentally demonstrated.

Instead, the article should distinguish:

**Established findings**

from

**Computational frameworks**

from

**Hypotheses about psychosis**

from

**Open questions**

from

**PIF as an original hypothesis.**

# X. PIF Research Position

The existing literature makes precision a scientifically legitimate
topic for investigation.

However, the literature does not establish PIF.

The appropriate research question is:

> Could instability in precision allocation or maintenance help explain
> cross-domain disturbances in schizophrenia?

This remains a hypothesis.

The project should actively compare PIF with competing explanations.

---

# XI. Reference Quality Note

The bibliography prioritizes:

1. peer-reviewed primary research;
2. major theoretical papers;
3. high-quality reviews;
4. recent reassessments of influential hypotheses.

Where a paper is primarily theoretical, the article should identify
it as theoretical rather than describing its claims as established
empirical findings.
