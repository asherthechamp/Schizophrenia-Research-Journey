# Article 04 — References

## What Is Predictive Processing?

This reference file contains the principal sources used for Article 04
of the Schizophrenia Research Journey.

The references are organized by their role in the article rather than

# 1. Foundational Predictive Coding

### Rao & Ballard (1999)

Rao, R. P. N., & Ballard, D. H. (1999).

Predictive coding in the visual cortex: A functional interpretation of
some extra-classical receptive-field effects.

*Nature Neuroscience, 2*, 79–87.

DOI: 10.1038/4580

Role in Article 04:

Foundational computational model of predictive coding in the visual
cortex. The model proposed that feedback connections carry predictions
while feedforward connections carry residual errors.

Source:

Nature Neuroscience

# 2. Predictive Processing Framework

### Clark (2013)

Clark, A. (2013).

Whatever next? Predictive brains, situated agents, and the future of

DOI: 10.1017/S0140525X12000477

Cambridge University Press.

# 3. Empirical Evaluation of Predictive Coding

### Hodson, Mehta & Smith (2024)

Hodson, R., Mehta, M., & Smith, R. (2024).

The empirical status of predictive coding and active inference.

*Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews, 157*, 105473.

DOI: 10.1016/j.neubiorev.2023.105473

Role in Article 04:

Provides a recent systematic evaluation of empirical evidence for
predictive coding and active inference.

Important finding:

Existing empirical evidence for predictive coding provides modest
support, while some findings can also be explained by alternative
feedforward models.

The review emphasizes the importance of testing specific computational
and neural claims rather than treating "predictive processing" as one
single theory.

---

# 4. Predictive Processing and Psychosis

### Sterzer et al. (2018)

Sterzer, P., Adams, R. A., Fletcher, P., Frith, C., Lawrie, S. M.,
Muckli, L., Petrovic, P., Uhlhaas, P., Voss, M., & Corlett, P. R.
(2018).

The Predictive Coding Account of Psychosis.

*Biological Psychiatry, 84*(9), 634–643.

DOI: 10.1016/j.biopsych.2018.05.015

Role in Article 04:

Major review connecting predictive coding and Bayesian inference with
psychosis.

Discusses possible relationships between prior beliefs, sensory
signals, prediction errors, and psychotic symptoms.

Important caution:

The paper reviews and evaluates a theoretical account; it does not
establish predictive coding as the cause of psychosis.

# 7. Recent Reassessment of Predictive Processing

### Furutachi & Hofer (2026)

Furutachi, S., & Hofer, S. B. (2026).

Rethinking Predictive Processing.

*Annual Review of Neuroscience, 49*, 471–494.

DOI: 10.1146/annurev-neuro-102124-031410

Role in Article 04:

Provides the most recent major reassessment used in this article.

The review examines the historical development of predictive
processing and evaluates evidence concerning sensory prediction-error
signals.

A central issue is that apparently similar prediction-error responses
may arise from fundamentally different underlying computations.

This source is particularly important for maintaining scientific
caution in the article.

# 8. Conceptual Background: Unconscious Inference

### Helmholtz

Helmholtz, H. von.

Historical work on unconscious inference and perception.

Role in Article 04:

Provides historical background for the idea that perception involves
inference rather than simple passive registration of sensory
information.

Note:

Helmholtz's work predates modern predictive-processing theory and
should be treated as an intellectual precursor rather than evidence
for modern predictive coding.

# 10. Bayesian Inference

Bayesian inference provides a mathematical framework for combining
prior information with evidence.

Basic formulation:

P(hypothesis | evidence)
∝
P(evidence | hypothesis) × P(hypothesis)

Role in Article 04:

Provides mathematical background for understanding why predictive
processing is often discussed alongside Bayesian inference.

Important distinction:

Bayesian inference is not identical to predictive processing.

Bayesian inference is a mathematical framework.

Predictive processing is a broader family of computational and
neuroscientific theories.

---

# 11. Free-Energy Principle and Active Inference

Predictive processing is frequently discussed alongside Karl Friston's
free-energy principle and active inference.

These frameworks overlap but should not be treated as synonymous.

For Article 04, the distinction is intentionally brief.

A more detailed examination should occur in a later article if the
curriculum requires it.

# 12. Evidence Classification

## Relatively Strong Evidence

- Prior information can influence perception.
- Context can influence perception.
- Prediction-related processes occur in the nervous system.
- Unexpected information can influence learning and behavior.

## Influential Theoretical Frameworks

- Predictive coding.
- Predictive processing.
- Hierarchical generative models.
- Bayesian approaches to perception.

## Active Research Questions

- Exact neural implementation of predictive coding.
- Nature of cortical prediction-error signals.
- Precision-weighting mechanisms.
- Relationship between predictive processing and psychosis.

## Not Established

- Predictive processing as a complete theory of brain function.
- Predictive processing as a complete theory of schizophrenia.
- Hallucinations as simply the result of overly strong priors.
- One predictive-processing abnormality explaining all psychotic
  symptoms.

# 13. PIF Relevance

The Precision Instability Framework is a proposed hypothesis developed
within the broader research journey.

Predictive processing provides a conceptual framework in which
precision can influence inference.

However:

Predictive processing
≠
PIF

PIF proposes that instability in the estimation, maintenance, or
allocation of precision may contribute to disturbances across multiple
psychological domains.

This hypothesis must be tested empirically and compared with competing
models.

---

# 16. Citation Discipline

The article should distinguish carefully between statements that are:

- empirical findings;
- computational models;
- theoretical interpretations;
- hypotheses;
- speculative extensions.

The phrase:

"consistent with predictive processing"

should not automatically be converted into:

"proves predictive processing."

Similarly:

"predictive-processing account of psychosis"

should not be converted into:

"predictive processing causes psychosis."
