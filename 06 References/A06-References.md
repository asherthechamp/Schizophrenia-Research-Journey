# Key concepts supported by the literature

## Precision

In many statistical models, precision is represented as the inverse of variance:

**Precision = 1 / Variance**

Higher precision corresponds to lower estimated uncertainty.

Precision should not be treated as synonymous with truth or accuracy.

---

## Precision-weighted prediction error

Predictive-processing models can represent prediction errors as weighted according to their estimated precision.

Conceptually:

**Precision × Prediction Error**

This represents computational weighting rather than a claim that the brain literally performs a simple multiplication in a single location.

---

## Attention and precision

Some predictive-processing theories propose relationships between attention and the allocation or modulation of precision.

Attention should not, however, be treated as simply identical to precision.

---

# Schizophrenia and psychosis

The literature provides theoretical and empirical reasons to investigate altered precision weighting in psychosis.

However, the evidence does not justify reducing schizophrenia to a single computational abnormality such as:

**"too much precision"**

or

**"too little precision."**

Potential mechanisms may involve different combinations of:

- prior beliefs;
- sensory evidence;
- prediction errors;
- uncertainty;
- precision weighting;
- attention;
- hierarchical inference;
- learning;
- context.

The direction and level of any abnormality may also differ across symptoms and experimental paradigms.

---

# Precision Instability Framework (PIF)

PIF is **not an established scientific theory**.

It is a proposed research hypothesis developed within the Schizophrenia Research Journey.

The central question is:

> Could instability in the maintenance, allocation, or updating of precision contribute to unstable inference in schizophrenia or psychosis?

The hypothesis requires empirical testing.

It must be distinguished from:

- ordinary neural noise;
- measurement noise;
- normal adaptive flexibility;
- transient changes in uncertainty.

A scientifically useful version of PIF must generate predictions that could potentially be falsified.

---

# Evidence hierarchy

## Established / foundational

- Precision as a statistical concept.
- The relationship between precision and variance.
- Bayesian representations of uncertainty.
- Predictive-processing models incorporating precision-weighted prediction errors.

## Active research

- Neural implementation of precision.
- Relationship between attention and precision.
- Hierarchical precision.
- Precision weighting in psychosis.
- Sensory versus prior precision.
- Temporal dynamics of precision.

## Proposed

- Precision instability as a potentially important computational property in schizophrenia and psychosis.

---

# Scientific caution

The Article 06 discussion should avoid claiming:

> "Schizophrenia is caused by abnormal precision."

It should instead state that altered precision weighting is one of several computational hypotheses being investigated in psychosis.

Likewise, PIF should be presented as a hypothesis intended to generate testable predictions, not as an established explanation.
