## How These References Support Article 12

The references are organized around several central ideas:

- **Cognitive control is goal-directed regulation**, rather than a single mental operation.
- **Goal maintenance** allows behavior to remain aligned with task demands over time.
- **Selection and competition management** determine which representations or actions receive priority.
- **Inhibition** is one component of control rather than the definition of control.
- **Monitoring and error processing** allow the system to detect mismatches between intended and actual outcomes.
- **Cognitive flexibility** allows control policies and task representations to change when circumstances change.
- **Proactive and reactive control** describe different temporal modes of maintaining and deploying control.
- **Prefrontal cortex participates in distributed networks** supporting cognitive control rather than acting as a single isolated control center.
- **Schizophrenia involves heterogeneous abnormalities** in cognitive control and associated neural systems; these findings do not establish one universal "control deficit."
- **Computational models** provide a bridge between cognitive mechanisms, behavior, and neural implementation.
- **Active-inference approaches** provide one contemporary theoretical framework for connecting control with precision and decision-making, but should not be treated as an established complete explanation of schizophrenia.

## Scientific Guardrails

This article should not be interpreted as claiming that:

1. Cognitive control is located entirely in the prefrontal cortex.
2. Cognitive control and executive function are universally identical concepts.
3. Inhibition is synonymous with cognitive control.
4. One cognitive-control task measures one pure underlying mechanism.
5. Schizophrenia is simply a "cognitive-control disorder."
6. All people with schizophrenia have the same cognitive-control abnormalities.
7. A behavioral deficit automatically identifies its neural mechanism.
8. Network abnormalities by themselves prove a specific psychological mechanism.
9. Computational models constitute established causal explanations of schizophrenia.
10. Precision-based or active-inference theories are established explanations of all cognitive-control abnormalities.

## Connection to the Schizophrenia Research Journey

Article 11 examined:

**Inhibition → How does the brain stop something that it has already started?**

Article 12 expands the question:

**Cognitive Control → How does the brain organize and maintain behavior around a goal despite competition, conflict, distraction, errors, and changing circumstances?**

This creates the next conceptual transition:

**Cognitive Control → Internal Cognition**

The next major question is:

> How does the brain control cognition when the signals it must regulate are generated from within?

## Note on Evidence

The references include reviews, theoretical papers, computational models, meta-analytic work, and schizophrenia-focused research.

They should be interpreted according to their study designs and theoretical assumptions. A theoretical model, neural association, behavioral finding, and meta-analysis do not provide the same type of evidence.

The goal of this reference collection is therefore not simply to accumulate papers, but to connect each conceptual claim in Article 12 to an appropriate level of scientific evidence.
