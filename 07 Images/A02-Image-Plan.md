# Article 02 — Visual Concept

## Article

**The Phenomenology of Schizophrenia**

## Core Visual Question

> What happens when the organization of experience begins to change?

## Central Concept

The image represents the transition between:

**Brain → Experience**

Rather than depicting schizophrenia as simply a damaged brain, the visual should communicate the relationship between biological processes and subjective experience.

The image should suggest that neuroscience can observe biological activity, while phenomenology asks what that activity becomes as lived experience.

## Visual Metaphor

A human figure / brain is positioned at the boundary between two domains:

### Objective Domain

- brain
- neural activity
- biological structure
- measurable signals
- scientific observation

### Subjective Domain

- perception
- thought
- self
- agency
- meaning
- emotional experience
- world-model

The transition between the two domains should appear continuous rather than divided by an absolute boundary.

## Scientific Message

The image must NOT imply:

- that schizophrenia is simply brain damage;
- that one brain region causes schizophrenia;
- that self-disorder is the established cause of schizophrenia;
- that hallucinations have a single known mechanism;
- that PIF is established science.

Instead, it should communicate:

> Understanding schizophrenia requires connecting biological mechanisms with lived experience.

## Relationship to Article 1

Article 1 asked:

> What is schizophrenia?

Article 2 asks:

> What is schizophrenia like from the inside?

Therefore, the visual progression should move from the more **diagnostic/biological framing of Article 1** toward the **subjective/phenomenological framing of Article 2**.
