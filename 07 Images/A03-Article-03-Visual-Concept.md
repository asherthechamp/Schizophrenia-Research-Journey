# Article 03 — Visual Concept

## How Does the Brain Construct Reality?

### Core visual idea

The image represents perception as an interaction between incoming
sensory evidence and internally generated expectations.

The visual sequence should communicate:

**Sensory evidence → Prediction → Inference → Perception → Experience**

The purpose is to illustrate an inferential process without implying
that the brain simply invents or "hallucinates" reality.

---

## Scientific message

Perception is not a passive recording of the external world.

Sensory systems provide incomplete and noisy signals. The nervous
system combines those signals with prior information, context, and
expectations to construct perceptual representations.

Predictive-processing theories provide one computational framework
for describing this process.

The image therefore represents **constrained inference**, not fantasy.

---

## Visual sequence

### 1. Sensory Evidence

External information enters through sensory systems.

Examples:

- light;
- sound;
- touch;
- other sensory signals.

The representation should emphasize that sensory information is
real but incomplete and potentially noisy.

↓

### 2. Prediction

The brain generates expectations based on previous experience,
context, and learned structure.

Predictions should be shown as feedback or top-down information.

↓

### 3. Inference

Sensory evidence and predictions interact.

The system evaluates their relative consistency and uncertainty.

Prediction error can signal a mismatch between expectation and
incoming evidence.

↓

### 4. Perception

The result is a perceptual interpretation of the available evidence.

This should not be portrayed as either:

"pure sensory recording"

or

"pure imagination."

Instead, it is a constructed representation constrained by evidence.

↓

### 5. Experience

Perception contributes to the conscious experience of the world.

The final stage should feel continuous with the earlier stages rather
than suggesting that experience is created independently of sensory
evidence.

---

## Important scientific constraint

Avoid visual language suggesting:

> "The brain hallucinates reality."

That phrase can be rhetorically attractive but scientifically
misleading if interpreted literally.

The image should instead communicate:

> **The brain constructs perceptual interpretations through the
> interaction of sensory evidence, prior expectations, and inference.**

---

## Relationship to schizophrenia

The image should NOT imply:

**Predictive processing = schizophrenia**

or

**prediction error = psychosis**

or

**strong priors = hallucinations**

Instead, the image establishes the normal computational problem:

> How does a nervous system infer what is happening from uncertain
> sensory information?

Article 3 then asks whether abnormalities in this process could
contribute to some features of psychosis.

---

## Relationship to PIF

PIF should not dominate the visual.

The image establishes the conceptual foundation from which the
later PIF hypothesis emerges.

The eventual conceptual progression is:

**Sensory evidence**

↓

**Prediction**

↓

**Inference**

↓

**Perception**

↓

**Experience**

↓

**Uncertainty**

↓

**Precision**

↓

**Psychosis**

↓

**PIF hypothesis**

PIF is an original proposed research hypothesis, not an established
scientific theory.

---

## Suggested image caption

**How does the brain construct experience from incomplete sensory
evidence?**

Perception emerges from the interaction of sensory signals,
predictions, context, and inference—not from sensory input alone.

---

## Accessibility description

A conceptual scientific illustration showing the progression from
sensory evidence through prediction and inference to perception and
conscious experience. The diagram emphasizes interaction between
bottom-up sensory information and top-down expectations while avoiding
the implication that the brain simply invents reality.

---

## Evidence status

The visual represents a conceptual synthesis of established and
actively researched ideas in neuroscience and computational
psychiatry.

It does not represent a validated model of schizophrenia.

PIF remains a proposed research hypothesis.
