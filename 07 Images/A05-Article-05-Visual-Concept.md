# Article 05 — Visual Concept

## What Is Prediction Error?

### Core visual idea

The image represents prediction error as a discrepancy between
expectation and incoming evidence.

The central conceptual sequence is:

**Prediction → Sensory Evidence → Mismatch → Prediction Error → Updating**

The visual should communicate that prediction error is informative
rather than simply a mistake or malfunction.

---

## Scientific Message

A prediction error is a computational description of a discrepancy
between an expected state, event, or outcome and the information
actually observed.

Prediction error can provide information that contributes to learning,
inference, and model updating.

The visual should therefore avoid portraying prediction error as a
literal "error" inside the brain.

---

## Visual Sequence

### 1. Prediction

Top-down information represents an expectation generated from prior
information, context, or an internal model.

↓

### 2. Sensory Evidence

Bottom-up information represents incoming sensory evidence.

The sensory evidence should appear real but incomplete and potentially
uncertain.

↓

### 3. Comparison

Prediction and sensory evidence meet.

The comparison should reveal a discrepancy.

↓

### 4. Prediction Error

The mismatch becomes a distinct computational signal.

It should be visually noticeable without looking like an alarm,
malfunction, or failure.

↓

### 5. Updating

The prediction-error signal contributes to updating the internal model.

↓

### 6. New Prediction

The system generates a revised expectation.

The cycle can continue.

---

## Conceptual Cycle

**Prediction**

↓

**Sensory Evidence**

↓

**Comparison**

↓

**Prediction Error**

↓

**Updating**

↓

**New Prediction**

---

## Important Scientific Constraint

Do NOT represent prediction error as:

- a brain malfunction;
- a literal error message;
- hallucination;
- schizophrenia;
- dopamine itself;
- surprise itself.

The image should communicate:

> Expectation meets evidence → discrepancy → information → updating.

---

## Prediction Error vs Surprise

The visual should not imply that prediction error and subjective
surprise are identical.

Prediction error concerns a discrepancy between expectation and
observation.

Surprise concerns unexpectedness and can be described using
information-theoretic concepts.

They are related but distinct.

---

## Prediction Error vs Reward Prediction Error

The image should primarily represent prediction error in the context
of inference and sensory evidence.

It should not imply that sensory prediction error and reward prediction
error are the same biological process.

---

## Relationship to Psychosis

Schizophrenia or psychosis should NOT dominate the visual.

Article 05 establishes the computational concept before applying it to
psychosis.

The eventual conceptual progression is:

**Prediction Error**

↓

**Precision**

↓

**Inference**

↓

**Salience**

↓

**Psychosis**

---

## Relationship to PIF

PIF should remain mostly implicit in the visual.

The image should establish the foundation:

**Prediction**

↓

**Evidence**

↓

**Prediction Error**

↓

**Updating**

↓

**Precision?**

The final question provides a transition into Article 06.

PIF is a proposed research hypothesis and should not be represented as
an established mechanism.

---

## Visual Style

Conceptual scientific illustration.

Clean, modern, intellectually serious.

Avoid:

- cartoon brains;
- medical horror imagery;
- red warning/error symbols;
- literal computer error messages;
- psychedelic hallucination imagery;
- overly technical diagrams.

Prefer:

- elegant signal flows;
- layered information;
- subtle visual contrast between prediction and evidence;
- a clear but non-alarming representation of mismatch;
- visual continuity between prediction and updating.

---

## Suggested Caption

**When expectation meets evidence, a mismatch can become information.**

Prediction errors describe discrepancies between what a system expects
and what it observes—discrepancies that can contribute to learning and
updating.

---

## Accessibility Description

A conceptual neuroscience illustration showing an interaction between
a top-down prediction and bottom-up sensory evidence. The two signals
meet at a comparison stage where a mismatch produces a prediction-error
signal, which then contributes to model updating and a new prediction.
The image represents prediction error as informative discrepancy rather
than brain malfunction.

---

## Evidence Status

The visual represents established computational concepts together with
active theoretical research in predictive processing and computational
psychiatry.

It does not represent a validated neural mechanism of schizophrenia.

PIF remains a proposed research hypothesis.
