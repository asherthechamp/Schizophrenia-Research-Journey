# LinkedIn / Facebook Post

How does the brain construct reality?

We often imagine perception as a simple process:

The world exists → sensory information enters the brain → we experience reality.

But the brain receives incomplete, noisy, and constantly changing signals.

Perception therefore involves more than recording.

The brain must interpret.

It combines sensory evidence with expectations, context, previous experience, and estimates of uncertainty.

This leads to a deeper question:

**What happens when the processes involved in inference and precision become altered?**

In Article 3 of The Schizophrenia Research Journey, we explore:

• Bayesian inference  
• predictive processing  
• prediction error  
• uncertainty  
• precision  
• hallucinations  
• delusions  
• aberrant salience  
• computational psychiatry

We also introduce the Precision Instability Framework (PIF).

But importantly, PIF is presented as a **proposed research hypothesis—not an established theory of schizophrenia.**

The goal is not to force schizophrenia into one explanation.

The goal is to develop hypotheses that can be tested against evidence and competing explanations.
