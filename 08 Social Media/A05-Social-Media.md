# LinkedIn

## Post

What happens when the brain's predictions don't match reality?

That mismatch is often described as **prediction error**.

But prediction error isn't simply a "mistake."

It can be information.

When expected and observed outcomes diverge, the discrepancy can help
the brain update its internal model of the world.

This idea appears across computational neuroscience and learning
theory—from predictive coding to reinforcement learning.

But the concepts shouldn't be collapsed into one another.

Prediction error is not simply:

dopamine.

surprise.

hallucination.

or schizophrenia.

The more interesting question is:

**How much should a prediction error matter?**

A discrepancy from an unreliable source may deserve little weight.

A smaller discrepancy from a highly reliable source may deserve much
more.

That takes us toward one of the most important concepts in predictive
processing:

**precision.**

In the fifth article of the Schizophrenia Research Journey, we explore
prediction error, dopamine, psychosis, aberrant salience, and the
proposed Precision Instability Framework.

# X / Twitter — Thread

## 1/8

What happens when the brain expects one thing but encounters another?

A traffic light changes unexpectedly.

A familiar sound suddenly changes.

An expected reward doesn't arrive.

The mismatch matters.

This is the basic intuition behind **prediction error**.

## 2/8

Prediction error isn't simply a "mistake."

It describes a discrepancy between an expectation and an observation.

That discrepancy can provide information.

The brain can use it to update its model of what is happening.

## 3/8

A simplified cycle is:

Prediction

↓

Observation

↓

Mismatch

↓

Prediction error

↓

Updating

↓

New prediction

The cycle can continue.

## 4/8

But not all prediction errors are the same.

Sensory prediction errors and reward prediction errors belong to
different computational frameworks.

They shouldn't automatically be treated as one biological mechanism.

## 5/8

Dopamine is strongly associated with reward-prediction-error models.

But dopamine is NOT simply "the prediction-error chemical."

Dopamine participates in multiple processes, including learning,
motivation, movement, and behavioral selection.

## 6/8

Prediction error also connects to theories of psychosis.

Researchers have asked whether altered weighting of predictions,
sensory evidence, uncertainty, and prediction errors could contribute
to hallucinations, delusions, or aberrant salience.

These remain active research questions.

## 7/8

And here's the deeper question:

**How much should a prediction error matter?**

A noisy signal may deserve little weight.

A highly reliable signal may deserve much more.

This is where **precision** enters the picture.

## 8/8

The fifth article in the Schizophrenia Research Journey explores
prediction error, learning, dopamine, psychosis, and the proposed
Precision Instability Framework.

Next:
