# The Schizophrenia Research Journey
## Project Governance

Version: 1.0
Status: Active
Scope: 50-Article Research Journey

---

# 1. Mission

The Schizophrenia Research Journey is a long-form scientific education and research project exploring schizophrenia through clinical psychology, psychiatry, neuroscience, computational neuroscience, computational psychiatry, genetics, neurobiology, artificial intelligence, and related disciplines.

The project aims to:

- Explain established scientific knowledge clearly.
- Connect findings across levels of analysis.
- Identify unresolved scientific questions.
- Compare competing explanatory models.
- Develop and examine original research hypotheses.
- Maintain a transparent distinction between evidence and speculation.

The project is educational and exploratory. Original hypotheses developed within the project must not be presented as established scientific conclusions.

---

# 2. Scientific Integrity Principle

The central rule of the repository is:

> Established scientific findings must remain clearly separated from interpretations and original hypotheses.

Every substantive claim should be assigned an evidence status where appropriate.

## Evidence Categories

### Established Finding

Supported by substantial and converging scientific evidence.

### Strongly Supported

Supported by considerable evidence but still subject to meaningful limitations.

### Plausible Interpretation

A reasonable synthesis or interpretation of existing evidence that remains open to revision.

### Open Question

A question for which the scientific evidence remains insufficient or conflicting.

### Proposed Hypothesis

An original or speculative explanation that requires empirical testing.

---

# 3. Precision Instability Framework

The Precision Instability Framework (PIF) is an original research hypothesis developed within this project.

PIF must always be represented as:

> A proposed research hypothesis.

PIF must NOT be represented as:

- An established theory of schizophrenia.
- A validated clinical model.
- A proven mechanism.
- A consensus scientific explanation.
- A clinically established diagnostic framework.

When discussing PIF, distinguish clearly between:

1. Established literature concerning precision, predictive processing, uncertainty, attention, inference, and related concepts.
2. Interpretations derived from that literature.
3. The original PIF hypothesis.

The purpose of this distinction is to allow PIF to be evaluated scientifically rather than treated as established fact.

# 5. Article Lifecycle

Each article moves through the following stages:

## Stage 1 — Planned

The article exists in the curriculum.

## Stage 2 — Research

Scientific literature is collected and analyzed.

## Stage 3 — Knowledge Integration

Reusable concepts and findings are added to the Knowledge Base.

## Stage 4 — Draft

The article is written.

## Stage 5 — Scientific Review

Claims, terminology, evidence strength, citations, and logical reasoning are reviewed.

## Stage 6 — Editorial Review

The article is checked for:

- Clarity
- Structure
- Accessibility
- Repetition
- Tone
- Readability

## Stage 7 — Publication Preparation

Images, references, title, subtitle, formatting, and social-media material are prepared.

## Stage 8 — Published

Only the final publication version belongs in:

05 Published Articles/

# 7. Agent Architecture

The project uses a manual agentic workflow.

## Agent 1 — Curriculum Planner

Responsibilities:

- Maintain the 50-article roadmap.
- Identify dependencies between articles.
- Adjust sequencing when new research requires it.
- Prevent unnecessary duplication.

## Agent 2 — Research Scientist

Responsibilities:

- Search scientific literature.
- Identify landmark studies and recent evidence.
- Compare competing theories.
- Assess evidence quality.
- Identify unresolved questions.
- Produce research dossiers.

The Research Scientist must distinguish primary research, systematic reviews, meta-analyses, expert reviews, and general informational sources.

## Agent 3 — Knowledge Base

Responsibilities:

- Maintain reusable concepts.
- Track definitions.
- Connect mechanisms across articles.
- Maintain terminology consistency.
- Prevent contradictions between articles.

## Agent 4 — Writer

Responsibilities:

- Convert research dossiers into accessible articles.
- Preserve scientific accuracy.
- Avoid overstating evidence.
- Clearly identify hypotheses.
- Maintain continuity across the series.

## Agent 5 — Scientific Reviewer

Responsibilities:

- Verify scientific claims.
- Check citations.
- Identify unsupported conclusions.
- Detect overgeneralization.
- Separate evidence from interpretation.
- Check whether original hypotheses are being presented appropriately.

## Agent 6 — Editorial/Publication Agent

Responsibilities:

- Prepare final article formatting.
- Develop image requirements.
- Prepare social-media content.
- Check publication readiness.

# 8. Evidence and Citation Policy

Scientific claims should be supported by appropriate sources.

Priority should generally be given to:

1. Original peer-reviewed research.
2. Systematic reviews and meta-analyses.
3. Major consensus statements.
4. High-quality expert reviews.
5. Authoritative institutions.
6. General educational sources when appropriate.

Sources should be evaluated for:

- Scientific quality
- Relevance
- Recency
- Methodological strength
- Potential conflicts
- Whether the source actually supports the claim being made

Never cite a source merely because its title appears relevant.

---

# 9. Claim Discipline

Avoid transforming:

"Study X found an association"

into:

"Mechanism X causes schizophrenia."

Avoid transforming:

"Model X proposes..."

into:

"Schizophrenia works through X."

Avoid transforming:

"Researchers have observed..."

into:

"Science has proven..."

The strength of language should match the strength of evidence.

---

# 10. Primary Research vs Interpretation

Every article should distinguish:

## What researchers observed

The empirical findings.

## What researchers concluded

The interpretation offered by the investigators.

## What the project concludes

Our synthesis of the literature.

## What the project proposes

Original hypotheses developed within this research journey.

These should never be silently merged.

---

# 11. Knowledge Base Rules

Knowledge Base entries should be:

- Concise.
- Reusable.
- Evidence-oriented.
- Linked to sources where appropriate.
- Updated when important new evidence appears.

If later evidence contradicts an existing Knowledge Base entry, update the entry rather than silently creating contradictory information elsewhere.

# 13. Publication Rule

An article may move from:

04 Article Drafts/

to:

- Scientific review
- Citation review
- Editorial review
- Image preparation
- Final proofreading

---

# 14. Original Research Ideas

Original ideas should be documented separately from established knowledge.

For each proposed hypothesis, record:

- Motivation
- Existing evidence
- Assumptions
- Mechanism
- Predictions
- Potential falsifiers
- Alternative explanations
- Evidence required to test it
- Current evidence status

A hypothesis should become stronger through testing, not through repeated citation of itself.

---

# 15. Scientific Humility

The project should explicitly acknowledge uncertainty.

Preferred language includes:

- "Evidence suggests..."
- "Research indicates..."
- "One interpretation is..."
- "A possible explanation is..."
- "This remains controversial..."
- "This hypothesis predicts..."
- "Evidence is currently insufficient..."

Avoid unnecessary certainty.

---

# 16. Reproducibility

Where practical, research claims should be traceable to:

- Original papers
- DOI or stable identifiers
- Datasets
- Methods
- Analytical approaches

When computational analyses are eventually performed, code and analysis documentation should be stored in the repository whenever possible.

---

# 17. Relationship to Clinical Practice

The project is a research and educational endeavor.

It should not present original models as clinical diagnostic or treatment recommendations.

Clinical claims should be grounded in appropriate medical evidence and authoritative guidance.

# 19. The Core Question

The project repeatedly returns to one overarching scientific question:

> How does biology become experience?

This question connects molecular neuroscience, neural circuits, computation, cognition, subjective experience, and behavior.

It is the conceptual thread running through the Schizophrenia Research Journey.

---

# 20. Governance Principle

The project should prefer:

Evidence over assertion.

Mechanism over labels.

Questions over premature conclusions.

Falsifiable hypotheses over speculation.

And scientific curiosity over certainty.
